In short: the Canadian citizenship test has 20 questions, each multiple choice or true or false, in English or French. You have 45 minutes and need at least 15 correct answers (75%) to pass. You get 3 chances within the 30-day window in your invitation. Most applicants aged 18 to 54 take it online.

If you've applied for Canadian citizenship, the test is one of the last steps between you and the ceremony. It's also smaller than many people expect: twenty questions, all drawn from one free government guide. This post covers the format, who has to take it and how the online version works, using only what Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) publishes on canada.ca.

Who has to take the citizenship test?

In most cases you take the test if you're between 18 and 54 years old on the day you sign your application. You don't take it if you're under 18 or 55 and over, or if you asked for and received a waiver. (IRCC: How the test works)

What kind of questions are on the test?

The test checks what you know about Canada. IRCC says it asks about the rights and responsibilities of citizenship and about Canada's history, geography, economy, government, laws and symbols. Every question is based on the official study guide, Discover Canada, so there's no surprise material from outside it. (IRCC: Study for the test)

To get a feel for the style, here's one of the sample questions from the guide itself: "What is the meaning of the Remembrance Day poppy?" The answer is to remember the sacrifice of Canadians who have served or died in wars up to the present day.

One thing the test does not do is grade your English or French. If you're invited to an interview later, the officer will note how well you communicate in one of the official languages, but that's separate from the test.

How long is the test, and can you pause it?

You have 45 minutes. Once the timer starts it can't be stopped, you get a warning when 5 minutes are left, and the test submits itself when time runs out, finished or not. Because you can't pause, set aside the full 45 minutes in a quiet place before you start. (IRCC: Take the online test)

Online, in person or on Microsoft Teams

IRCC invites most applicants to take the test online, from anywhere, using a link in the invitation. You don't even need to be in Canada. Your webcam stays on and takes random photos to confirm it's you and that you're working alone.

If you need an accommodation, IRCC arranges another format instead: an in-person test, written on paper or taken orally (other formats such as Braille are possible), or an oral test over a Microsoft Teams video call. In every format you get the same 3 chances, in English or French.

What you need for the online test

  • ID: your permanent resident card (even if it's expired), or a valid photo ID with your signature, such as a driver's licence or health card.
  • A computer or tablet with a webcam: a desktop, laptop or tablet using Chrome or Safari. You can't use a phone, Chrome on an iPad or a Microsoft Surface Pro.
  • No VPN, a reliable internet connection and no other tabs, windows or programs open.
  • A good spot: enough light for the webcam to see you clearly, and nothing in the background with your personal information on it (like a diploma). A public library computer is allowed.
  • Your application number and UCI (unique client identifier) to sign in.

During the test you must be alone, stay where you took your photo, and not use other devices, study material or your browser's back button. Breaking these rules, or a technical problem that stops IRCC confirming your identity, can make your result invalid.

How you get your result

After an online test, the screen shows your score straight away. That score is unofficial, and you can't sign in again to see it, so print the page or use the email button before you close it. An officer then reviews the test to make the result official, which can take a few days or a few weeks. If you took the test in person or on Teams, you get your official score right after the test. (IRCC: Test results and next steps)

The official result is one of three things: pass (at least 15 of 20), fail (you failed every attempt in your 30-day window) or invalidated (suspected cheating or a technical issue). After a pass, IRCC may invite you to an interview, and then invites you to the citizenship ceremony.

How many chances do you get?

Three, within the 30-day period in your invitation. If you fail all three, IRCC invites you to a hearing with a citizenship official. We cover that in what happens if you fail the Canadian citizenship test. To avoid getting there, read our 4-week Discover Canada study plan.
