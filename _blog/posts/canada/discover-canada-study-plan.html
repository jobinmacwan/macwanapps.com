In short: all citizenship test questions come from Discover Canada, the free official guide. Study it for 20 to 30 minutes a day over 4 weeks: three weeks reading and quizzing yourself a few chapters at a time, and a final week of full practice tests. Most people get their test invitation 1 to 3 months after IRCC acknowledges their application, so the best time to start is now.

The Canadian citizenship test has 20 questions and a pass mark of 15, and IRCC says plainly where the questions come from: "We base all the test questions on information found in the study guide." That guide is Discover Canada: The Rights and Responsibilities of Citizenship. Learn it well and there's nothing else to study. Here's a plan that gets through it in four weeks without cramming.

Why Discover Canada is the only book you need

IRCC calls Discover Canada "the only official study guide for the citizenship test", and it's free. You can read it online, download the PDF or eBook, listen to the audio version or order a paper copy. IRCC also warns that if you use other material, "you do so at your own risk". That includes apps like ours, so whatever you practise with, check it against the guide. (IRCC: Discover Canada)

When to start studying

Start when you apply, not when the invitation arrives. IRCC says most people get their test invitation 1 to 3 months after the acknowledgment of receipt (AOR), sometimes longer, and the invitation gives you a 30-day window to take the test. You can't book the test yourself, so the only way to be ready on time is to be ready early. (IRCC: Wait for a test invitation)

What's in the guide

The online edition has these sections. The first few (the oath, a message to readers and how to apply) are short; the test questions come mostly from the ten in the middle:

  • Rights and Responsibilities of Citizenship
  • Who We Are
  • Canada's History
  • Modern Canada
  • How Canadians Govern Themselves
  • Federal Elections
  • The Justice System
  • Canadian Symbols
  • Canada's Economy
  • Canada's Regions

The 4-week Discover Canada study plan

Plan on 20 to 30 minutes a day, five or six days a week. Each week covers a few chapters, grouped so related topics sit together.

WeekReadPractise
Week 1Rights and Responsibilities of Citizenship · Who We Are · Canada's HistoryA short quiz after each chapter. History is the longest section, so give it three or four days.
Week 2Modern Canada · How Canadians Govern Themselves · Federal Elections · The Justice SystemQuiz each chapter, then redo week 1's quizzes. Government questions are easy to mix up, so slow down here.
Week 3Canadian Symbols · Canada's Economy · Canada's Regions, plus your own province or territoryQuiz each chapter, then a first full 20-question practice test.
Week 4Only the chapters behind your wrong answersA 20-question practice test most days. Review every mistake the same day.

The daily loop matters more than the exact schedule: read a section, quiz yourself straight away, and go back over what you got wrong. Re-reading feels productive, but testing yourself is what makes facts stick on test day.

Learn the facts that have changed since 2012

Discover Canada was published in 2012, and some facts in it have changed. The clearest example is the Sovereign: the guide's sample questions still mention "the Queen", but Canada's head of state is now His Majesty King Charles III. (Canadian Heritage: The Monarch)

The guide also asks about things that depend on you. Its own study questions include "Who is your member of Parliament?" and "What is the capital of the province or territory that you live in?" Look these up for where you live, along with your premier and your province's or territory's representative of the Crown, and add them to your notes. (Discover Canada: Study Questions)

Use the guide's own study questions

The last section of the guide is a page of study questions that IRCC says are "similar to the questions that are found on the citizenship test". Use them twice: once at the start to see how questions are worded, and again in week 4 to check you can answer every one without looking.

If reading is hard, listen

If long reading sessions don't work for you, the official audio version of Discover Canada lets you get through chapters on a commute or a walk. Listening counts, as long as you still quiz yourself afterwards.

How you know you're ready

You're ready when you pass full 20-question practice tests comfortably and consistently, not just once. The real pass mark is 15, so a few practice scores of 17 or 18 in a row give you a buffer for nerves.
