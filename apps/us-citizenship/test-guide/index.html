The U.S. civics test: 2025 vs 2008, and which one you'll take

Two civics tests are in use right now. Your N-400 filing date decides which one is yours.

Facts checked October 23, 2026 · Jobin Macwan · 5 min read

At your naturalization interview, a USCIS officer tests two things: your English (reading, writing and speaking) and your knowledge of U.S. history and government, the civics test. Since October 2025 two versions of the civics test have been in use at the same time, so it's worth knowing which one is yours before you start studying.

Which test is yours

It depends on when you filed Form N-400, not on your interview date:

  • Filed on or after October 20, 2025: the 2025 civics test.
  • Filed before October 20, 2025: the 2008 civics test.

The 2025 civics test

  • The officer asks up to 20 questions from a list of 128.
  • You pass with 12 correct.
  • The officer stops as soon as you have answered 12 correctly, or 9 incorrectly.

The 2008 civics test

  • The officer asks up to 10 questions from a list of 100.
  • You pass with 6 correct.
  • The officer stops after 6 correct or 5 incorrect answers.

It's an interview, not a multiple-choice test

The officer asks each question out loud and you answer in your own words; there are no options to choose from. Any answer on USCIS's accepted list for that question counts. Some answers change with elections and appointments, such as the President or your senators, and you must give the name of the official serving at the time of your interview. A few questions also depend on where you live, like your state's senators, governor and capital.

The 65/20 special consideration

If you are 65 or older and have been a lawful permanent resident for 20 years or more, you study a shorter list: 20 specially marked questions from your version of the test. The officer asks 10 of them, and you may take the civics test in the language of your choice.

The English test

  • Reading: read 1 of 3 sentences aloud correctly.
  • Writing: write 1 of 3 sentences correctly.
  • Speaking: the officer judges this during your interview.

USCIS publishes reading and writing vocabulary lists to study from.

If you don't pass

You get one more chance. USCIS retests you only on the part you didn't pass, English or civics, between 60 and 90 days after your first interview. If you don't pass the second time, the application is denied.

How to prepare

  1. Confirm your version from your N-400 filing date.
  2. Study from USCIS's free materials at uscis.gov/citizenship: the question list for your version, the study guide and the vocabulary lists.
  3. Practise answering out loud, without options in front of you, the way the interview works.
  4. Look up the answers that depend on elections and on your state, and check them again shortly before your interview.
  5. Practise reading and writing short sentences using the official vocabulary.

Frequently asked questions

Which civics test will I take, 2025 or 2008?
It depends on your Form N-400 filing date. Filed on or after October 20, 2025: the 2025 test. Filed before that date: the 2008 test. Your interview date doesn't change it.
How many questions must I get right?
On the 2025 test, 12 of up to 20 questions, from a list of 128. On the 2008 test, 6 of up to 10, from a list of 100. The officer stops once you've passed or can no longer pass.
Can I take the civics test in my own language?
If you qualify for the 65/20 special consideration (65 or older and a lawful permanent resident for 20 years or more), you may take the civics test in the language of your choice. Otherwise the test is in English.
What happens if I fail the citizenship test?
You get a second chance on the part you failed, English or civics, between 60 and 90 days after your first interview. Failing again leads to denial of the application.
Do I need to know my state's officials?
Yes. Some questions ask about your state, such as its senators, governor and capital, and answers about officials must name whoever is serving at the time of your interview.
