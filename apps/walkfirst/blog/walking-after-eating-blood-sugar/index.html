Walking after eating: does a 10-minute walk lower blood sugar?

November 24, 2026 · Jobin Macwan · 5 min read

In short: yes. A short walk soon after a meal lowers the rise in blood sugar that follows it. In a 2016 trial of adults with type 2 diabetes, a 10-minute walk after each main meal lowered post-meal blood sugar by about 12% compared with one 30-minute walk a day, and by about 22% after dinner. Even light walking breaks during long periods of sitting help. It's a habit, not a treatment, so talk to your doctor if you manage diabetes.

"Go for a walk after dinner" sounds like something a grandparent would say. It turns out the research backs them up. Here's what the studies actually found, why the timing matters, and how to make the after-meal walk stick.

What the research found

The best-known study is a randomised crossover trial published in Diabetologia in 2016. Researchers at the University of Otago had 41 adults with type 2 diabetes try two routines, each for two weeks:

  • a 10-minute walk after each of the three main meals, starting within 5 minutes of finishing eating, or
  • one 30-minute walk at any time of day.

The total walking time was the same, 30 minutes a day. But blood sugar after meals was about 12% lower when people split it into after-meal walks. Most of the difference came from dinner: walking after the evening meal, usually the most carbohydrate-heavy and followed by the most sitting, cut the post-meal rise by about 22%. (Reynolds et al., 2016; University of Otago)

Even short, easy walks help

You don't need a power walk. A 2022 review in Sports Medicine pooled seven randomised trials that broke up long periods of sitting with either standing or light-intensity walking. Compared with sitting still, light walking breaks significantly lowered both blood sugar and insulin after meals. Standing helped a little; walking helped more. (Buffey et al., 2022)

Timing matters too. A 2023 meta-analysis in the same journal found that exercising after a meal lowered blood sugar spikes more than exercising before eating, and concluded that walking helps most when you do it as soon as possible after the meal. (Sports Medicine, 2023)

Why a walk after eating works

After a meal, sugar from your food moves into your blood. Working muscles use sugar for fuel, so when you walk while that sugar is arriving, your legs take some of it up and the peak is lower. Sitting still after a large meal, the classic evening on the sofa, gives the sugar nowhere to go.

How to make the after-dinner walk a habit

  1. Start with dinner. It's the meal where the trial saw the biggest effect, and the one most often followed by hours of sitting.
  2. Go within a few minutes of finishing. The trial started walks within 5 minutes. Put your shoes by the door so leaving takes no decision.
  3. Ten minutes is enough. A comfortable pace counts. Around the block twice is a good start.
  4. Have a bad-weather version. Walk a few laps of your building, the stairs or a mall. The point is moving, not the scenery.
  5. Make it the price of the evening scroll. If the after-dinner hour usually disappears into your phone, use that: walk first, scroll after.

Three short walks a day also add up to 30 minutes, which is most of the way to the weekly amount health guidelines recommend. More on that in does walking count as exercise?

Who should check with a doctor first

Walking is safe for most people, but if you have diabetes and take insulin or other medicine that can cause low blood sugar, or you have heart disease or another chronic condition, ask your doctor how post-meal activity fits your plan. The CDC advises people with chronic conditions to talk with their doctor about the types and amounts of activity that are right for them. (CDC) This post is general information, not medical advice.

Frequently asked questions

How long should you walk after eating to lower blood sugar?
Ten minutes after each main meal was enough in a 2016 trial of adults with type 2 diabetes, and reviews suggest even shorter light walking breaks help. Starting within a few minutes of finishing your meal seems to matter more than walking for a long time.
Is it better to walk before or after a meal?
After. A 2023 meta-analysis found exercise after a meal lowered blood sugar spikes more than exercise before eating, and that walking helps most when done as soon as possible after the meal.
Does walking after dinner help more than after other meals?
In the 2016 trial, yes. Walking after the evening meal cut the post-meal rise by about 22%, the largest effect of any meal, likely because dinner was the most carbohydrate-heavy and was followed by the most sitting.
Is a slow walk after eating enough?
Light-intensity walking still helped in the studies reviewed. Walking lowered post-meal blood sugar and insulin compared with sitting, and more than standing did.
