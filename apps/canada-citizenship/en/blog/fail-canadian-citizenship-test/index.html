What happens if you fail the Canadian citizenship test?

November 24, 2026 · Jobin Macwan · 5 min read

In short: one failed attempt doesn't end your application. You get up to 3 chances to pass within the 30-day period in your invitation. If you fail all three, IRCC invites you to a hearing with a citizenship official, which can include an oral version of the test (20 questions, 15 to pass). If you fail the hearing, your application is refused, and you'd have to reapply and pay the fees again.

Seeing "fail" on a test you've been waiting months for is a horrible feeling. The good news is that the Canadian process expects some people to need more than one try. Here's exactly what happens next, step by step, based on what Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) publishes.

One failed attempt is not the end

Your test invitation gives you a 30-day period, and within it you have up to 3 chances to pass, whether you test online, on Microsoft Teams or in person. The pass mark is the same each time: at least 15 of 20 questions right. (IRCC: Test results and next steps)

The score you see on screen after an online test is unofficial. An officer reviews it before it becomes official, which can take a few days or weeks. IRCC only records an official "fail" when you've failed every attempt in your 30-day window.

Plan your three attempts. Because all three chances sit inside the same 30 days, don't leave your first attempt to the last week. Starting early leaves you time to study between attempts.

Failed or invalidated? They're different

A result can also be invalidated. That happens if IRCC finds or suspects cheating, or if a technical issue stops them confirming your identity or your score: a blurred photo, a dropped connection, another tab open. If that happens, IRCC follows up and asks you to take the test again. While a result is invalidated or still under review, your application status shows In progress. (IRCC: Take the online test)

IRCC doesn't send email updates about your result, so check your application status in the online tracker.

What happens at the citizenship hearing

If you fail all three attempts, IRCC invites you to a hearing with a citizenship official. It lasts 30 to 90 minutes and helps IRCC decide whether you're eligible for citizenship. Depending on the type of hearing, the officer may ask you questions out loud:

  • About your knowledge of Canada, similar to the test. This oral knowledge test has 20 questions, and you must get 15 right.
  • About your residence in Canada.
  • To assess your English or French. The officer can ask up to 9 questions, and you must pass 6.

Afterwards, IRCC sends you a letter with the result. If you pass the hearing, you wait for your invitation to the citizenship ceremony.

If you fail the hearing

If you fail the hearing, IRCC refuses your application. To become a citizen you'd need to apply again and pay the fees again. That's why it pays to treat every attempt, and the hearing, as seriously as the first.

If you applied as a family

IRCC processes family or group applications together. If one person fails, it may be possible to separate the applications so others can go to their ceremony. To ask, email the address in the "Rescheduling" section of your invitation.

How to pass on your next attempt

All the questions come from one source, Discover Canada, so a failed attempt usually means a few chapters weren't solid yet, not that the test is unfair. What we'd suggest:

  1. Write down what you remember. Straight after the attempt, note the topics you were unsure about: dates, government, a region.
  2. Re-read only those chapters. Don't start the whole guide again. Fix the weak spots first.
  3. Practise by chapter, then mixed. Quiz yourself on one chapter until it's easy, then take full 20-question practice tests that mix every topic, like the real one.
  4. Aim above the pass mark. Practice scores of 15 leave no room for nerves. Wait until you pass practice tests comfortably and consistently before your next attempt.
  5. Check the facts that change. The guide was published in 2012. For example, Canada's head of state is now His Majesty King Charles III. See our Discover Canada study plan for more.

Frequently asked questions

How many times can you take the Canadian citizenship test?
You get up to 3 chances to pass within the 30-day period in your test invitation. If you fail all three, IRCC invites you to a hearing with a citizenship official.
What happens at a citizenship hearing?
A citizenship official may ask you questions out loud about your knowledge of Canada (20 questions, 15 to pass), about your residence in Canada, and to check your English or French (up to 9 questions, 6 to pass). It lasts 30 to 90 minutes.
What happens if you fail the citizenship hearing?
IRCC refuses your application. You would need to apply again and pay the fees again to become a citizen.
Is an invalidated test the same as a failed test?
No. A result can be invalidated if IRCC suspects cheating or a technical issue stops them confirming your identity or score. IRCC then follows up and asks you to take the test again.
Is the score I see after the online test my final result?
No. The on-screen score is unofficial. An officer reviews your test to make the result official, which can take a few days or a few weeks.
