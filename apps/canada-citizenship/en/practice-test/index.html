10 sample questions with answers

One question from each chapter of Discover Canada. Try each one before you open the answer.

  1. Rights and Responsibilities of Citizenship

    What personal benefits of volunteering does the guide mention?

    1. Guaranteed job placement in the public service
    2. Gaining useful skills and building friendships and contacts
    3. Tax credits and reduced tuition fees
    4. Faster processing of citizenship applications
    Show the answer

    Answer: B. Gaining useful skills and building friendships and contacts. Volunteering is presented as an excellent way to develop useful skills and form friendships and contacts.

    In Discover Canada: Rights and Responsibilities of Citizenship — Citizenship Responsibilities

  2. Who We Are

    What was the name of Canada's original constitutional document, passed in 1867?

    1. The Constitution Act, 1982
    2. The Quebec Act
    3. The British North America Act
    4. The Statute of Westminster
    Show the answer

    Answer: C. The British North America Act. The British North America Act of 1867 was Canada's original constitutional document, and it is the source of the phrase 'Peace, Order and Good Government'. The other acts are real but date from different periods.

    In Discover Canada: Who We Are — Introduction

  3. Canada's History

    Who were the United Empire Loyalists?

    1. Settlers loyal to the Crown who left the new United States for British North America
    2. French colonists who swore an oath of allegiance to the British Crown after the Conquest of 1759
    3. British soldiers who stayed in Canada after the War of 1812
    4. Members of the first legislative assemblies of the Canadas
    Show the answer

    Answer: A. Settlers loyal to the Crown who left the new United States for British North America. More than 40,000 people who remained loyal to the Crown fled the American Revolution and settled mainly in Nova Scotia and Quebec; they are known as Loyalists.

    In Discover Canada: Canada's History — United Empire Loyalists

  4. Modern Canada

    By the 1960s, what proportion of Canadians traced their origins to somewhere other than Britain or France?

    1. Two-thirds
    2. One-quarter
    3. One-half
    4. One-third
    Show the answer

    Answer: D. One-third. By the 1960s one-third of Canadians traced their origins to somewhere other than Britain or France, which gave fresh momentum to the idea of multiculturalism.

    In Discover Canada: Modern Canada — A Changing Society

  5. How Canadians Govern Themselves

    What is one advantage of Canada's federal structure?

    1. Municipalities can set their own foreign policy
    2. Each province can shape its own approach and try out new policies
    3. The federal government can overturn the result of any provincial election
    4. All provinces are required to adopt identical laws
    Show the answer

    Answer: B. Each province can shape its own approach and try out new policies. Federalism lets a province design policy around the people who actually live there, and gives it room to test fresh ideas instead of being locked into one national formula.

    In Discover Canada: How Canadians Govern Themselves — Federal state

  6. Federal Elections

    What are elected provincial legislators called in Quebec?

    1. Members of the National Assembly (MNAs)
    2. Members of the Legislative Assembly (MLAs)
    3. Members of Provincial Parliament (MPPs)
    4. Members of the House of Assembly (MHAs)
    Show the answer

    Answer: A. Members of the National Assembly (MNAs). Quebec's provincial legislature is the National Assembly, so its members are MNAs. MLAs sit in most other provinces and territories, MPPs in Ontario, and MHAs in Newfoundland and Labrador.

    In Discover Canada: Federal Elections — Other Levels of Government

  7. The Justice System

    In a Canadian criminal case, the accused person carries the responsibility of proving their own innocence.

    1. True
    2. False
    Show the answer

    Answer: False. Because of the presumption of innocence, a person is regarded as innocent until guilt is proven — the accused does not have to demonstrate innocence.

    In Discover Canada: The Justice System — Introduction

  8. Canadian Symbols

    The Canadian coat of arms can be spotted on dollar bills, government documents, and public buildings.

    1. True
    2. False
    Show the answer

    Answer: True. The coat of arms appears widely in everyday life — on currency, official government papers, and public buildings across the country.

    In Discover Canada: Canadian Symbols — Coat of Arms and Motto

  9. Canada's Economy

    The hydro-electric dam featured in the chapter stands on the Saguenay River, located in which province?

    1. Quebec
    2. British Columbia
    3. Ontario
    4. Manitoba
    Show the answer

    Answer: A. Quebec. The Saguenay River flows through Quebec, and the dam on it illustrates hydro-electricity's place among Canada's energy resources.

    In Discover Canada: Canada's Economy — photo captions

  10. Canada's Regions

    Which city is the capital of Nova Scotia?

    1. Fredericton
    2. Sydney
    3. Halifax
    4. Dartmouth
    Show the answer

    Answer: C. Halifax. Halifax is Nova Scotia's capital as well as Canada's largest port on the east coast.

    In Discover Canada: Canada's Regions — Regions and capitals

What the test covers

IRCC says the test asks about the rights and responsibilities of citizens and about Canada's history, geography, economy, government, laws and symbols. In Discover Canada that is ten chapters:

  • The rights and responsibilities of citizenship
  • Who we are
  • Canada's history
  • Modern Canada
  • How Canadians govern themselves
  • Federal elections
  • The justice system
  • Canadian symbols
  • Canada's economy
  • Canada's regions

The test doesn't assess your English or French. If you are invited to an interview, the officer notes how well you communicate in one of the official languages.

How to use a practice test well

  1. Read the explanation every time, even when you got it right. It tells you which part of the guide the fact lives in.
  2. Note the chapters you miss and go back to those pages of Discover Canada instead of memorising single answers.
  3. Take several different rounds. A score on questions you have already seen measures memory, not readiness. Aim for 17 or more on fresh sets.
  4. Finish with a timed run of 20 questions in 45 minutes, so the clock on the day holds no surprises.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the Canadian citizenship test?
20 questions, either multiple choice or true or false, in English or French. The test is 45 minutes long.
What is the pass mark for the Canadian citizenship test?
You need at least 15 correct answers out of 20 (75%). You have up to 3 chances to pass within the 30-day period in your test invitation.
Are these the questions from the actual test?
No. IRCC doesn't publish its test questions. These are practice questions written from Discover Canada, the guide IRCC bases every test question on, and each answer names the section of the guide it comes from.
Is this practice test free?
Yes. It is free with no sign-up and no limit on rounds. Each round draws 20 questions from a sample of 200.
What score should I aim for before the test?
The pass mark is 15 of 20. Aim to score 17 or more on several different sets of questions before your test, so a few unfamiliar questions on the day don't put you below the line.
