The Canadian citizenship test in 2026: how it works and how to pass

Twenty questions, forty-five minutes, fifteen to pass. Everything IRCC says about the test, from the invitation email to your results, in one place.

Facts checked 24 November 2026 · Jobin Macwan · 7 min read
  • 20 questions, multiple choice or true or false
  • 45 minutes, in English or French
  • Pass mark: 15 of 20 (75%)
  • Up to 3 chances within the 30-day period in your invitation
  • Who takes it: applicants aged 18 to 54 on the day they sign their application
  • What to study: Discover Canada, IRCC's free study guide

The citizenship test is the step most applicants worry about, and it is shorter than its reputation. Every question is based on one free guide, and you get three chances to pass. Here is how it works, based on Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada's (IRCC) own pages.

Who has to take the test

In most cases, you take the test if you are between 18 and 54 years old on the day you sign your citizenship application. You don't take it if you are under 18 or 55 or older, or if you asked for and received a waiver.

When your invitation arrives

You can't book the test yourself. IRCC emails you an invitation, and most people get it 1 to 3 months after they receive the acknowledgment of receipt (AOR) of their application. Non-routine applications can take longer.

  • The invitation is a PDF attached to an email from an address ending in @cic.gc.ca or @canada.ca, so check your junk folder.
  • It gives a start date and an end date 30 days apart, a link to the test, your application number, your unique client identifier (UCI) and an email address for rescheduling.
  • You can take the online test at any time in those 30 days, weekends and holidays included.
  • The "Citizenship test" section of the application status tracker shows In progress once your test is scheduled.

What's on the test

The test has 20 questions, each either multiple choice or true or false, in English or French. IRCC bases every question on the study guide Discover Canada: The Rights and Responsibilities of Citizenship. The questions cover the rights and responsibilities of citizens and Canada's:

  • history
  • geography
  • economy
  • government
  • laws
  • symbols

The test doesn't assess your English or French. If you are later invited to an interview, the officer notes how well you communicate in one of the official languages. The guide is free to read online, download as a PDF or eBook, listen to as audio, or order on paper, and IRCC publishes its own study questions.

Taking the test online

IRCC invites most applicants to the online test. It is 45 minutes long, and once the timer starts you can't pause it. You get a warning with 5 minutes left, and at 45 minutes the test is submitted automatically, finished or not. You don't need to be in Canada to take it.

Before you start

  • ID: your permanent resident card (even if expired), or a valid piece of ID with your photo and signature, such as a driver's licence or health card.
  • Device: a desktop, laptop or tablet with a webcam, using Chrome or Safari. Not a phone, not Chrome on an iPad, not a Microsoft Surface Pro, and not over a VPN.
  • Place: good lighting so the webcam sees you clearly, nothing with your personal information in the background, and a reliable connection. A public library computer is allowed.
  • Sign-in: your application number and UCI. You then request a test link by email; it expires after 15 minutes, so be ready before you ask for it.

During the test

  • The webcam takes random photos to confirm it's you and that you're alone.
  • Close every other tab, window and program, and don't use another device.
  • No study materials, and don't press your browser's back button.
  • Stay in the place where you took the photos of yourself and your ID.

Technical problems or signs of cheating can make a test invalid, and IRCC then asks you to take it again.

If you need an accommodation

If you need one, IRCC arranges the test in person (written on paper, or oral, and in other formats such as Braille) or as an oral test on Microsoft Teams. You can ask for an accommodation in your application or after you receive your invitation. Every format has the same 3 chances and the same choice of English or French.

Your results

When you submit the online test, the screen shows your score straight away. That score is unofficial, and IRCC doesn't email it to you or let you sign in to see it again, so print it or email the page to yourself before you close it. An officer then reviews the test to make the result official, which can take a few days or a few weeks. The official result is one of three:

  • Pass: at least 15 of the 20 questions right.
  • Fail: you failed every attempt in your 30-day period.
  • Invalidated: suspected cheating, or a technical issue that stopped IRCC from confirming your identity or score.

IRCC doesn't send email updates about this; watch the status tracker. For tests taken in person or on Teams, you get the official score right after the test.

If you fail

Within your 30-day period you have up to 3 chances to pass. If you fail, sign in again and use the next one. If you fail all 3, IRCC invites you to a hearing with a citizenship official, which lasts 30 to 90 minutes. The official may ask you questions orally about your knowledge of Canada (an oral test of 20 questions, with 15 needed to pass), about your residence in Canada, or to assess your English or French. If you fail the hearing, the application is refused, and you would need to apply and pay the fees again.

If you miss the test

If you miss the 30-day window the first time you're invited online, IRCC sends a new invitation automatically, and you still get 3 chances. If you miss a second invitation, email the address in the "Rescheduling" section with a detailed reason, your application number and your UCI. If you miss it twice and don't get in touch, IRCC stops processing your application. Rescheduling usually takes 4 to 8 weeks.

After you pass

Depending on your case, IRCC may invite you to an interview to verify your identity, check your original documents and confirm you are eligible. Bring the original documents you sent with your application and any passport or travel documents you used in the 5 years before you applied. After that comes the invitation to the citizenship ceremony, where you take the Oath of Citizenship.

A study plan that works

  1. Read or listen to Discover Canada once, start to finish. Don't memorise yet; get the shape of it.
  2. Study one chapter at a time, then answer questions on that chapter only. Chapters show you where you're weak; full tests hide it.
  3. Check every mistake against the guide instead of memorising the answer, so a differently worded question doesn't catch you out.
  4. Take full practice tests under the clock: 20 questions in 45 minutes, on question sets you haven't seen. Aim for 17 or more, a safe margin above 15.
  5. The week before, set up for the day: computer or tablet, Chrome or Safari, webcam, ID, a quiet bright room, and 45 minutes nobody will interrupt.

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are on the Canadian citizenship test?
20 questions, each either multiple choice or true or false. The test is 45 minutes long.
What is the pass mark?
At least 15 correct answers out of 20, which is 75%.
How many times can I take the citizenship test?
You have up to 3 chances to pass within the 30-day period in your invitation. If you fail all 3, IRCC invites you to a hearing with a citizenship official.
Can I take the citizenship test in French?
Yes. The test is available in English or French, in every format. Our guide is also in French.
Do I have to be in Canada to take the online test?
No. IRCC says you don't need to be in Canada to take the online test, but you must take it alone, on a computer or tablet with a webcam, using Chrome or Safari, without a VPN.
When will I get my test invitation?
Most people get it 1 to 3 months after they receive the acknowledgment of receipt (AOR) of their application. It comes by email as a PDF, from an address ending in @cic.gc.ca or @canada.ca.
