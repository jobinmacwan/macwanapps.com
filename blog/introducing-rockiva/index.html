Introducing Rockiva: point your camera at a rock, learn what it is

July 7, 2026 · Jobin Macwan · 3 min read

TL;DR — Rockiva is out now on the App Store. Point your camera at any rock, crystal, or gemstone and get a confidence-scored identification with real mineralogy — in 35 languages.

Everyone has done it: picked up an interesting stone on a beach or a trail and wondered — is this something? Quartz? Jasper? Just a pretty pebble? Unless you happen to hike with a geologist, that curiosity usually dies right there.

Rockiva answers the question in seconds. Point your camera at any rock, crystal, or gemstone, and it identifies the specimen with real mineralogy, not just a guess — the mineral family, hardness, composition, and a confidence score so you know how sure the identification actually is.

Real mineralogy, honestly presented

Identification apps have a trust problem: many will confidently name anything you point at, even when the honest answer is "hard to tell from this photo." Rockiva does it differently. Every result comes with a confidence score, and the details are grounded in actual geology — Mohs hardness, chemical composition, where the mineral typically forms. When a photo isn't good enough for a solid identification, the app tells you that too.

Under the hood, Rockiva uses modern AI vision models with results streamed to your device in seconds. Your identification history stays local on your phone, and there's no account to create.

35 languages — including the AI itself

Here's the part I'm most proud of as an engineer: Rockiva is localized in 35 languages, and that includes the AI-generated identifications themselves, not just the buttons and menus. A rockhound in Japan, Brazil, or Germany gets the full mineralogy in their own language. Most apps translate the interface and stop; the actual content stays English. That always felt half-done to me.

Who it's for

Rock collectors checking a flea-market find. Parents whose kids fill their pockets with gravel treasures. Hikers, beachcombers, crystal enthusiasts, and anyone who's ever stood in a museum gift shop wondering what "labradorite" actually is. One honest note: for valuable specimens, treat Rockiva as a knowledgeable first opinion — a professional gemologist is still the final word.

Rockiva is available now on the App Store.
The rock world is more interesting than you think.
