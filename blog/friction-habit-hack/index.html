Friction is the only habit hack that ever worked for me

August 14, 2026 · Jobin Macwan · 5 min read

I've tried most of the habit canon: streaks, accountability partners, rewards, identity statements, apps that grow little trees. Everything worked for two weeks. Exactly one principle has kept working for years, and it's almost insultingly simple: behavior follows friction. You don't do what you intend to do — you do what's easiest to start.

The physics of it

Behavioral researchers have shown this repeatedly, in settings as mundane as cafeteria layouts: move an option a little closer and consumption rises, a little farther and it falls. Nobody's preferences changed — the friction did. Your day works the same way. Instagram is one thumb-length away; your running shoes are in a closet behind two doors. That, not character, is why one wins.

The liberating implication: you can stop trying to be a different person and start being an architect. Add steps to what you want less of. Remove steps from what you want more of.

Adding friction to the bad habit

In escalating order of strength: move the app off your home screen (search-only access adds two seconds of consciousness); log out after each session; delete the app and use the browser version, which is worse on purpose; or make access cost something real. Each tier catches the people the previous one didn't — doomscrolling in particular tends to laugh at the gentle tiers.

Removing friction from the good habit

Same logic, inverted. Shoes by the door, not the closet. Water bottle filled the night before. Walking clothes laid out. The gym bag already in the car. Every removed step raises the odds the habit starts, and starting is the whole battle — nobody abandons a walk at minute seven.

Connecting the two

The strongest design links them: the bad habit's friction becomes the good habit's fuel. That's the entire mechanism of WalkFirst — my distracting apps stay locked until I've hit my step goal, so the pull toward the feed converts directly into laps around the block. I'm not fighting the craving; I'm taxing it. Two years of failed habit systems, and the one that stuck is the one where scrolling pays for walking.

Motivation is weather. Friction is climate. Build for the climate.

Frequently asked questions

What is friction in habit formation?
Friction is the number of steps, seconds, and decisions between you and a behavior. Small increases in friction reliably reduce a behavior; small decreases increase it — often more powerfully than motivation or willpower.
How do I add friction to phone habits?
In escalating order: move distracting apps off your home screen, log out after each session, delete the app and use its worse browser version, or use a blocker that makes access conditional — for example on hitting a daily step goal.
Why do habit streaks fail?
Streaks add motivation but no structure: one missed day destroys the accumulated value and often the habit with it. Friction changes survive missed days because they live in your environment, not your memory or morale.
