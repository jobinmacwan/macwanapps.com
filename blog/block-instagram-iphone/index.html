How to block Instagram on iPhone — every method, honestly ranked

August 15, 2026 · Jobin Macwan · 5 min read

You can absolutely block Instagram on an iPhone. The real question — the one this guide actually answers — is how hard the block is to undo at 11pm, because that's when every method gets tested. Here's every option, ranked from easiest to bypass to hardest, with the honest catch for each.

Method 1: Screen Time app limit (easy to set, easy to break)

Settings → Screen Time → App Limits → add Instagram, pick a daily allowance. It works — until the limit hits and iOS offers "Ignore Limit" right on the shield. One tap and you're back in. You can blunt this by setting a Screen Time passcode (ideally one a partner holds) and choosing "Block at end of limit," but if you know the passcode, you've only added seconds. Best for: people who need a speed bump, not a wall.

Method 2: Downtime (good for nights)

Screen Time → Downtime schedules a window — say 9pm to 7am — where only apps you allow stay usable. It's the right shape for the bed-scrolling problem, since it covers both midnight and morning. Same weakness though: the passcode unlocks everything, and you probably know the passcode.

Method 3: Delete the app (stronger than it sounds, weaker than you hope)

Deletion adds real friction — reinstalling takes a minute, and that minute kills many impulses. Two catches: instagram.com works in Safari (block it under Screen Time → Content Restrictions if you go this route), and in my experience the demand usually reroutes to whatever scrollable app survives. You haven't ended the scroll; you've relocated it.

Method 4: Make access cost something (the one that held for me)

Third-party blockers use Apple's Screen Time framework to shield apps, and the good ones change the economics instead of just adding a lock you own the key to. This is the category WalkFirst lives in: Instagram stays shielded until I've hit my daily step goal — 50% of the goal buys 10 minutes, the full goal unlocks the day. There's no "ignore" button to argue with; there's a walk. The block holds precisely because undoing it produces the habit I actually wanted. Fair disclosure: I built WalkFirst, and honest disclosure: apps like it work best when you want the replacement habit, not just the absence of Instagram.

Which should you pick?

Speed bump → Screen Time limit with a partner-held passcode. Night protection → Downtime. Clean break → delete plus a web block. A block that builds something → step-gated blocking. Whatever you choose, the friction principle predicts the outcome: the method that wins is the one whose bypass costs more than the scroll is worth.

Frequently asked questions

Can you permanently block Instagram on iPhone?
Not in an absolute sense — every method on iOS can be undone by the phone's owner eventually. The practical goal is raising the cost of undoing: partner-held Screen Time passcodes, content restrictions for the website, or blockers whose unlock requires something like a step goal get closest.
Why doesn't my Screen Time limit for Instagram work?
Almost always because of the 'Ignore Limit' button, which appears when a limit is reached and costs one tap. Set a Screen Time passcode you don't know (a partner sets it), enable 'Block at end of limit,' and the limit gains real teeth.
How do I block Instagram Reels but keep the rest of Instagram?
iOS can't block one feature inside an app — blocking works at the app level. Your options are Instagram's own built-in time reminders (weak), blocking the whole app in windows when Reels tempt you most, or accepting the app-level block and using instagram.com for essentials.
